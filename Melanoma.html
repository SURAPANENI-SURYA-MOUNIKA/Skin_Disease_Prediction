Melanoma

What is it?

Melanoma is a serious and aggressive type of skin cancer that develops from melanocytes, the pigment-producing cells in the skin. It can spread (metastasize) to other organs if not detected early, making it one of the most dangerous forms of skin cancer.

Causes

UV Exposure: Prolonged sun exposure or tanning beds increase the risk. Genetics: Family history of melanoma raises susceptibility. Fair Skin: Lighter skin tones, freckles, and light-colored eyes have a higher risk. Many Moles (Nevi): Having numerous or atypical (dysplastic) moles. Weakened Immune System: Conditions like HIV/AIDS or immunosuppressive therapy. History of Sunburns: Severe childhood sunburns increase melanoma risk.

Symptoms

A – Asymmetry: One half of the mole does not match the other. B – Border Irregularity: Edges are uneven, jagged, or blurred. C – Color Changes: Multiple colors (black, brown, red, white, or blue). D – Diameter: Larger than 6mm (size of a pencil eraser). E – Evolving: Any change in size, shape, color, or symptoms like itching or bleeding.

Precautions

Avoid tanning beds and excessive sun exposure. Regular skin checks for early detection.

Doctor’s Advice

Surgical excision is required. Advanced cases need immunotherapy, chemotherapy, or radiation.
