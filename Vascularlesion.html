Vascular lesion

What is it?

A vascular lesion is an abnormal growth or malformation of blood vessels (veins, arteries, or capillaries) in the skin or internal organs. These lesions can be congenital (present at birth) or develop later in life due to various factors. Vascular lesions are generally benign (non-cancerous) but can sometimes cause cosmetic concerns or medical complications.

Causes

ongenital (Birth Defects): Many vascular lesions develop before birth. Genetic Factors: Family history may increase the risk. Hormonal Changes: Pregnancy, puberty, or hormonal therapy can trigger some vascular lesions. Aging: Some types, like cherry angiomas, appear with age. Sun Damage: Prolonged sun exposure may contribute to telangiectasia. Skin Trauma or Injury: Can trigger pyogenic granulomas or telangiectasia. Underlying Health Conditions: Liver disease, connective tissue disorders, or blood vessel abnormalities.

Symptoms

Skin Discoloration: Red, purple, or blue patches or spots on the skin. Raised or Flat Lesions: Some vascular lesions are flat (e.g., port-wine stains), while others are raised (e.g., hemangiomas, cherry angiomas). Swelling or Pain: Some vascular malformations can cause swelling, discomfort, or even ulceration. Bleeding: Certain lesions, like pyogenic granulomas, may bleed easily. Visible Veins: Spider veins or larger venous malformations may be prominent on the skin.

Precautions

Sun Protection: Use SPF 30+ sunscreen to prevent worsening of vascular lesions. Avoid Skin Trauma: Injuries can worsen existing lesions or trigger new ones. Regular Skin Checks: Monitor changes in size, color, or shape of vascular lesions. Manage Underlying Conditions: Control blood pressure, liver disease, or hormonal imbalances. Healthy Lifestyle: A balanced diet and hydration promote vascular health.

Doctor’s Advice

Surgical excision is required. Advanced cases need immunotherapy, chemotherapy, or radiation.
