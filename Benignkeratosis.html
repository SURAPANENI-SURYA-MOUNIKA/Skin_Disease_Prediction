Benign Keratosis

What is it?

Benign keratosis is a non-cancerous skin condition characterized by thickened, rough, or scaly skin growths. It includes several types, such as seborrheic keratosis, lichen planus-like keratosis, and solar lentigines. These growths are harmless but may resemble skin cancer, requiring proper diagnosis.

Causes

Aging: Common in middle-aged and older adults Sun Exposure: UV radiation can contribute to growth development Genetics: Family history increases the likelihood Skin Irritation or Inflammation: Can trigger keratotic changes Unknown Factors: Some types develop without clear causes

Symptoms

Waxy, wart-like growths that appear "stuck on" the skin. Colors range from light brown to black. Usually harmless but can become irritated.

Precautions

Seborrheic Keratosis: Waxy, wart-like, or "stuck-on" appearance Brown, black, or tan in color Can appear anywhere except palms and soles Lichen Planus-like Keratosis (LPLK): Reddish, scaly patch often mistaken for skin cancer May develop suddenly, sometimes after sun exposure Solar Lentigines (Liver Spots): Flat, brownish patches on sun-exposed areas (face, hands, arms)

Doctor’s Advice

Diagnosis: Physical examination and dermoscopy Biopsy if suspicious for malignancy Treatment (if necessary): Cryotherapy (Freezing): Liquid nitrogen removes growths Curettage & Electrosurgery: Scraping and cauterizing the lesion Laser Therapy: Removes pigmented lesions Topical Treatments: Retinoids or lightening agents for cosmetic concerns
