Tinea Ringworm Candidiasis

What is it?

Tinea, commonly known as ringworm, is a contagious fungal infection that affects the skin, scalp, nails, or feet. It appears as a red, circular, scaly rash with a raised border, resembling a "ring." Despite the name, it is not caused by worms.

Causes

Fungal Species: Dermatophytes (Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton) Spread by Contact: Direct skin contact with an infected person, animal, soil, or contaminated objects (towels, clothing, gym equipment). Warm & Humid Conditions: Excessive sweating, tight clothing, or poor hygiene increases risk. Weakened Immune System: More common in individuals with diabetes, HIV, or other immune disorders.

Symptoms

Circular, red, scaly rash with a clear center. Itchy, inflamed, and raised border. Cracking, peeling, or blistering skin (especially on feet). Brittle, thickened, or discolored nails (Tinea Unguium). Hair loss in patches (Tinea Capitis – scalp ringworm). Common Types of Tinea Tinea Corporis: Body ringworm (arms, legs, torso). Tinea Pedis: Athlete’s foot (feet, between toes). Tinea Capitis: Scalp ringworm (causes hair loss). Tinea Cruris: Jock itch (inner thighs, groin, buttocks). Tinea Unguium: Nail fungus (toenails or fingernails).

Precautions

Maintain Hygiene: Keep skin clean and dry. Avoid Sharing Personal Items: Towels, clothes, combs, and shoes. Wear Loose, Breathable Clothing: Helps prevent fungal growth. Change Socks & Underwear Daily: Especially after sweating. Use Antifungal Powder/Spray: Helps prevent recurrence. Treat Pets: Pets can carry and spread ringworm.

Doctor’s Advice

Diagnosis: Physical examination, skin scraping for fungal culture. Treatment: Topical Antifungals: Clotrimazole, Miconazole, Terbinafine. Oral Antifungals (Severe Cases): Griseofulvin, Itraconazole, Terbinafine. Medicated Shampoos (Scalp Infection): Ketoconazole, Selenium Sulfide.
