Actinic Keratosis

What is it?

Actinic keratosis (AK) is a precancerous skin condition caused by long-term exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun or tanning beds. It can progress into squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) if left untreated.

Causes

Chronic sun exposure (especially in fair-skinned people). Weakened immune system (HIV, organ transplant patients). Frequent use of tanning beds.

Symptoms

Rough, scaly, dry patches on sun-exposed areas (face, scalp, ears, hands). Lesions are pink, red, or brown with a sandpaper-like texture. Mild itching, tenderness, or a burning sensation.

Precautions

Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+) daily. Wear hats, sunglasses, and protective clothing. Avoid prolonged sun exposure, especially between 10 AM - 4 PM. Get regular dermatologist check-ups.

Doctor’s Advice

Treatment includes cryotherapy (freezing the lesion), topical chemotherapy (5-FU, imiquimod), photodynamic therapy, or laser therapy. If untreated, AK may develop into squamous cell carcinoma.
