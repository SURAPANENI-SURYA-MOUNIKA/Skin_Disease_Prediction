Squamous cell carcinoma

What is it?

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is a common type of skin cancer that develops in the squamous cells of the epidermis (outer skin layer). It is more aggressive than basal cell carcinoma and can spread to other parts of the body if left untreated.

Causes

Chronic Sun Exposure: UV rays from the sun or tanning beds are the primary cause. Fair Skin: Light-skinned individuals have a higher risk. Older Age: More common in people over 50. Weakened Immune System: Conditions like HIV, organ transplants, or immunosuppressive therapy increase risk. History of Skin Damage: Burns, scars, chronic wounds, or previous radiation therapy. HPV Infection: Certain strains of the human papillomavirus (HPV) are linked to SCC.

Symptoms

Scaly, red, or rough patches on sun-exposed areas (face, ears, hands, lips, scalp). Firm, raised bumps or nodules that may crust or bleed. Persistent sores or ulcers that do not heal. Thickened, wart-like growths. Pain, itching, or tenderness in affected areas.

Precautions

Use Sunscreen: Apply SPF 30+ daily, especially on exposed skin. Avoid Tanning Beds: UV exposure significantly increases SCC risk. Wear Protective Clothing: Hats, sunglasses, and long sleeves provide protection. Regular Skin Checks: Self-examine for new or changing lesions and consult a dermatologist yearly. Avoid Smoking: Smoking increases the risk of SCC, especially on the lips. Treat Precancerous Lesions: Actinic keratosis (AK) can develop into SCC, so early treatment is important.

Doctor’s Advice

Physical Examination & Dermoscopy to assess suspicious lesions. Skin Biopsy to confirm SCC. Imaging Tests (CT, MRI, PET) if spread is suspected. Treatment: Surgical Excision: The most common treatment; removes the cancerous tissue. Mohs Surgery: Precise removal for high-risk or facial SCC. Cryotherapy: Freezing small SCCs with liquid nitrogen. Radiation Therapy: Used for large or inoperable tumors. Topical Treatments: Creams like 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) or imiquimod for early SCC. Targeted Therapy & Immunotherapy: Used for advanced cases.
